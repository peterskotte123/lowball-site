San Diego rent report, September 2026

Are San Diego rents being cut this month?

Some are. From Sep 8 to Sep 30, 2026, 2,669 of the 13,255 San Diego County rental listings we followed (20%) lowered their asking price at least once. Among those 2,669 listings, the median cut was 4.2% of the asking price before the cut, or $125 a month. In the 3 complete seven-day periods that touch September, the share ran from 9% to 10%, out of 7,966 to 8,941 listings followed in each. These are cuts on listings that were up during the month. They are not a measure of what rents are overall or where they are heading.

How long do listings sit before a cut?

The share of listings that have cut rises with the days a listing has been shown on Zillow and peaks at 61 to 90 days, where 34% of 514 listings had cut, with a median total cut of 5.0%. In the first week it is 12% of 1,904. Among listings shown 31 to 60 days, the median further time up was 28 days. These shares come from 7,790 rental listings scored up to Oct 31, 2026, San Diego County plus ZIPs our users browse, and Zillow's day count restarts when a listing is relisted.

What does this mean for a renter?

These figures describe listings, not any one landlord. How is this measured?

We pull the San Diego County rental listings on Zillow across the 81 county ZIPs on a nightly schedule: 29 pulls from Sep 8, 2026 to Oct 31, 2026, and a missed night is filled from the next pull. A cut is a lower asking price on the same listing, dated by the listing's own price history when Zillow gives a date, else by the night we first saw the new price; the same change reported twice counts once. A listing is counted in a period when it was up at any point in it. The weekly chart uses complete seven-day periods counted from Sep 8. Asking prices are not rents signed, so this is not a measure of rent levels, and nothing here is a forecast.
