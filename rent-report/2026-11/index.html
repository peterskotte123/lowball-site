San Diego rent report, November 2026

15% of the 9,068 San Diego County rental listings we followed from Nov 1 to Nov 16, 2026, cut their asking price, by a median of 4.2%. Figures cover Nov 1 to Nov 16, 2026, the data so far this month.

Are San Diego rents being cut this month?

Some are. From Nov 1 to Nov 16, 2026, 1,339 of the 9,068 San Diego County rental listings we followed (15%) lowered their asking price at least once. Among those 1,339 listings, the median cut was 4.2% of the asking price before the cut, or $145 a month. In the 3 complete seven-day periods that touch November, the share ran from 7% to 9%, out of 6,965 to 8,570 listings followed in each. These are cuts on listings that were up during the month. They are not a measure of what rents are overall or where they are heading.

How long do listings sit before a cut?

The share of listings that have cut rises with the days a listing has been shown on Zillow and peaks at 61 to 90 days, where 34% of 516 listings had cut, with a median total cut of 5.0%. In the first week it is 12% of 1,904. Among listings shown 31 to 60 days, the median further time up was 28 days. These shares come from 7,795 rental listings scored up to Nov 16, 2026, San Diego County plus ZIPs our users browse, and Zillow's day count restarts when a listing is relisted.

What does this mean for a renter?

These figures describe listings, not any one landlord. A listing that has been up for weeks belongs to the group where cuts are most common, and a listing posted this week belongs to the group where they are least common. lowball.rent/check shows how long a single listing has really been up, including time before a relist, the cuts it has already taken, and what similar listings nearby are asking. How is this measured?

We pull the San Diego County rental listings on Zillow across the 81 county ZIPs on a nightly schedule: 34 pulls from Sep 8, 2026 to Nov 16, 2026, and a missed night is filled from the next pull. A cut is a lower asking price on the same listing, dated by the listing's own price history when Zillow gives a date, else by the night we first saw the new price; the same change reported twice counts once. A listing is counted in a period when it was up at any point in it. The weekly chart uses complete seven-day periods counted from Sep 8. Asking prices are not rents signed, so this is not a measure of rent levels, and nothing here is a forecast.
