What is a notice to vacate?

It is a short written statement telling your landlord that you are moving out and on what date. It ends a month-to-month tenancy, and on a fixed lease it tells the landlord you will leave when the term is up, which many leases require even though the end date is already in the lease. A good notice says five things: who you are, the address, the date you are leaving, where to send your deposit, and the date of the letter itself. It does not need a reason. The template above writes those into a letter you can copy into an email or print and sign. Tenants also search for this as a 30-day notice to landlord or a lease termination letter; they are the same document.

How much notice do you have to give your landlord?

Whatever your lease says, and never less than your state requires. Thirty days is the most common figure for a month-to-month tenancy, and some leases ask for 60. A few states set different periods, and some require that the notice end on the last day of a rental period, so 30 days given on the 10th may run to the end of the following month. Read the notice or termination section of your lease first, then check your state's rule. Our month-to-month lease guide lists what we found in each state's statutes. If your move-out date falls short of the required notice, the landlord can usually charge rent through the full period, so send the letter early.

How should you deliver the notice?

The way your lease says to. Many leases name a method, such as mail to a stated address, hand delivery to the office, or the resident portal, and a notice sent another way can be disputed. If the lease allows email, email is fine and gives you a dated record. If it requires paper, send it by a method that proves delivery, such as certified mail with a return receipt, or hand it in and ask for a dated copy signed by the office. Keep a copy of the letter and the proof either way. When more than one tenant is on the lease, put every name on the notice, because a notice from one roommate may not end the tenancy for the others.

What happens to your security deposit?

Giving a forwarding address in the notice is the step that protects it. Landlords generally must return the deposit, less lawful deductions, within a deadline set by state law, and many states require an itemized list of what was deducted. The deadline and the rules differ from state to state, so look up yours rather than relying on a general figure. Before you hand back the keys, clean the home, photograph every room with the date visible, and ask for a move-out inspection so that any problem is named while you can still fix it. Return all keys and get a receipt. This is general information, not legal advice.

Moving somewhere new?

The month before a move is when the next rent gets decided, and it is more negotiable than most people assume. A listing that has sat for weeks, or one that has already cut its price, often has room to come down, and a landlord would usually sooner take a little less than leave a home empty for another month.
