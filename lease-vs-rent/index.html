Lease vs rent: what is the difference?

A lease fixes your rent and your stay for a set term, usually a year. A rental agreement runs month to month, so you can leave on short notice and the rent can change on short notice. Here is how they compare for a tenant.

LeaseMonth-to-month rental agreement
LengthA set term, most often 12 monthsOne month at a time, renewing until someone gives notice
RentFixed for the term unless the lease itself allows a changeCan be raised after written notice
Ending itEnds on the date in the lease; leaving early usually costs moneyEither side can end it with notice, commonly 30 days
Changing the termsNot until the term ends, unless both sides agreeThe landlord can change terms after notice
Best whenYou plan to stay and want a known rentYou may move soon or are between plans
Usual priceThe lower monthly rentOften a higher rent or a monthly fee

What is the difference between a lease and a rental agreement?

The length of the commitment. A lease is a rental contract for a fixed term, most often twelve months, and it holds both sides to the rent and the rules for that term. A rental agreement, in the sense most landlords use it, runs month to month: it renews every month and either side can end it or change it by giving written notice. In everyday speech the words overlap, and a document titled "rental agreement" can turn out to be a twelve-month lease, so read the section on the term to see which one you are signing. "Renting" is simply what a tenant does under either one. Both are binding contracts, and both give you the same basic rights as a tenant.

Is it better to lease or rent month to month?

A lease is usually better if you intend to stay, and month to month is better if you may need to leave. A lease protects you from a rent increase for the whole term and is normally the cheaper option per month, because the landlord gets a year of certainty. Its cost is the commitment: moving out early can mean a fee, lost deposit or rent owed until a new tenant is found. Month to month gives the freedom to go on a month's notice, and the landlord has the same freedom to raise the rent or end the tenancy on notice. If you are waiting on a job, a house purchase or a move you cannot date, pay for the flexibility. Otherwise take the lease.

Can rent go up during a lease?

Usually not. A lease fixes the rent until the term ends, unless the lease contains a clause that allows an increase part-way through, so look for one before you sign. Under a month-to-month agreement the rent can rise after written notice. Of the 45 states we checked against their own statutes, 19 set a minimum notice period for that, from 30 to 90 days, and the rest leave it to the agreement. A handful of places also cap the size of an increase on covered units. Our month-to-month lease guide lists the notice rule in each state, and rent increase laws by state has the caps. What happens when a lease ends?

One of three things, and your lease says which. You sign a new lease, often at a new rent. You move out, having given whatever notice the lease requires. Or you stay without signing anything, and in many states the tenancy then carries on month to month on the same terms for as long as the landlord accepts rent. Some leases renew automatically for another full term unless you give notice by a deadline, so check for that clause well before the end date. The renewal offer is also a negotiation: the landlord would usually prefer a tenant who stays to an empty unit. Can you negotiate a lease before signing?

Yes. The rent on a listing is the landlord's asking price, and the term is one of the things you can trade. Offering a longer lease, a move-in date that suits the landlord, or proof of on-time rent can each be worth a lower monthly figure, and a listing that has been up for weeks or has already cut its price usually has more room.
