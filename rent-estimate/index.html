What is a rent estimate?

A rent estimate is a model's best guess at what a unit would rent for, built from what similar homes nearby are listed at. It is not the price: the landlord sets the asking rent, and the estimate only tells you whether that ask sits high, low or about in line with the neighborhood. It is also not what the unit will actually rent for. Many listings cut their price before they rent, and the final number is agreed between landlord and tenant, so neither the ask nor the estimate is the clearing price. Treat an estimate as a reference point you can check against real listings, not as an appraisal. A good one tells you how it was built, which listings stand behind it, and how often it has been wrong.

How does Zillow's Rent Zestimate work?

According to Zillow's own pages, a Rent Zestimate is Zillow's estimate of a property's monthly rent, computed with a proprietary formula and meant as a starting point rather than a fixed price. Zillow lists its inputs as the home's physical features, such as square footage, bedrooms and bathrooms, comparable rentals and market rents in the area, facts updated by the owner, and other public data such as the last sale price. An older Zillow Research post says models within each county or state learn from recent rental listings how home attributes relate to rents, then estimate rents for all homes, including ones not listed; the current model may differ. Zillow also shows a Rent Range, and a wide range signals less data or more volatility. Lowball works differently: it reads the listings on the market near the unit, using the rules below, plus the listing's own days listed and price cuts. Zillow's figure is never an input.

How accurate is a rent estimate?

Ours is usually within about 10% of the asking rent. For the model accepted as of 2026-10-25, the median error against asking rent is 8.2% in the typical ZIP: we take the median error within each of 148 ZIPs that have at least 30 listings, then the median across ZIPs. Every prediction is made by a model that did not train on that listing. The 80% range shown in the panel contains the asking rent 80.0% of the time. Measured on 16,638 listings. Asking rent is the only truth available at this scale, so a listing priced well above the market counts as error, which is the point. Error is larger in expensive and thinly listed areas. What is a rent comp?

A rent comp, short for comparable, is a listing similar enough to the one you are looking at that its rent says something about this one. Where Lowball builds comparables from listings on the fly, the rules are: the same number of bedrooms, the same class (a house is compared with houses, an apartment or condo with attached homes), within 5 miles, and within 35% of the unit's size when both sizes are known. Of those, the 8 nearest are kept. If fewer than 3 qualify, the search widens to one bedroom fewer and sizes within 60%, and the panel marks them as a looser match. Asks under half or over 2.2 times the group's median are dropped, which removes room shares and short stays.

Why would an estimate differ from the asking price?

Because the estimate cannot see everything the landlord priced in. Condition and finishes matter, and a renovated kitchen or a worn carpet barely shows in beds, baths and square feet. Photos, views and noise matter too. Timing matters: a listing posted at the start of a busy month may ask more than one that has sat. And some landlords simply start high and wait. A gap between the ask and the estimate is a starting point for a conversation, not proof the listing is overpriced. How do you get a rent estimate for one listing?

Paste the listing's Zillow link at lowball.rent/check, which works on a phone. On a computer, the free Lowball extension shows the same panel on any Zillow rental page as you browse. The panel gives the estimate with its range, the nearby listings behind it and what they ask, how long this listing has been up, whether it and listings near it have cut their price, an opening offer, and a short message you can send the landlord. The message cites only things the landlord can check on the listing or nearby ones, so the offer reads as a business case rather than a haggle.
