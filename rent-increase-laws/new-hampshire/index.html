How much can a landlord raise rent in New Hampshire?

New Hampshire has no statewide cap on rent increases, so state law does not limit the amount. What it does set is notice: at least 30 days in writing on a month-to-month tenancy.

Is there a limit on rent increases in New Hampshire?

No. New Hampshire sets no statewide limit on how much a landlord can raise rent. On a month-to-month tenancy the rent can go up by any amount once proper notice is given, and when a lease ends the landlord can offer a renewal at any rent.

Two limits apply everywhere: federal fair housing law bars an increase that discriminates, and many states bar raising rent to retaliate against a tenant for a complaint. An increase also cannot change the rent in the middle of a fixed-term lease unless the lease allows it.

How much notice does a landlord have to give in New Hampshire?

At least 30 days, in writing, on a month-to-month tenancy. A tenant's refusal to agree to a rent increase is good cause for eviction only if the landlord gave written notice of the amount and effective date of the increase at least 30 days before the effective date; for all residential tenancies, 30 days' eviction notice suffices. Source: RSA 540:2, IV; RSA 540:3, II.

Count the days from when you received the notice, and check your lease, which can require more notice than the statute does. A text or a phone call is not written notice where writing is required.

Can a city in New Hampshire pass rent control?

We did not find a statute on a primary source that either allows or bars local rent control in New Hampshire, so this page does not say. If your city has an ordinance, it will be in the municipal code; the state rules on this page apply either way.

Can rent go up during a lease in New Hampshire?

Usually not. A fixed-term lease sets the rent for the term, and the landlord cannot change it until the term ends unless the lease itself has a clause allowing a mid-term increase. The notice rules above matter when the term ends and a new term or a month-to-month tenancy begins. In New Hampshire: RSA 540:2, II(i): for a lease of 12 months or longer, the landlord may end it at expiration with at least 60 days' written notice of non-renewal. Read your lease for an escalation clause before assuming either way.

What can you do about a rent increase?

Start with the two checks on this page: was the notice in writing and long enough, and does your lease allow a change now. If the notice is valid, the question is whether the new rent is in line with the market, and that part is negotiable.
