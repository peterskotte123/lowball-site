How much can a landlord raise rent in Ohio?

Ohio has no statewide cap on rent increases, so state law does not limit the amount. It also sets no notice period specific to a rent increase, so the lease controls.

Is there a limit on rent increases in Ohio?

No. Ohio sets no statewide limit on how much a landlord can raise rent. On a month-to-month tenancy the rent can go up by any amount once proper notice is given, and when a lease ends the landlord can offer a renewal at any rent.

Two limits apply everywhere: federal fair housing law bars an increase that discriminates, and many states bar raising rent to retaliate against a tenant for a complaint. An increase also cannot change the rent in the middle of a fixed-term lease unless the lease allows it.

How much notice does a landlord have to give in Ohio?

The statute sets no notice for a rent increase; it says the landlord or tenant may terminate or fail to renew a month-to-month tenancy by notice given at least thirty days prior to the periodic rental date. Source: Ohio Rev. Code § 5321.17(B).

Count the days from when you received the notice, and check your lease, which can require more notice than the statute does. A text or a phone call is not written notice where writing is required.

Can a city in Ohio pass rent control?

No, apart from any narrow exception the statute itself names. State law bars cities and counties in Ohio from controlling rent. Ohio Rev. Code § 5321.19(A) (HB 430, eff. Sept. 23, 2022): no political subdivision may enact or enforce any measure regulating rights under a rental agreement, including by any way imposing or requiring rent control or rent stabilization; findings in § 5321.20.

So there is no city ordinance to look for: the state rules on this page are the whole of it, and 23 other states we checked take the same approach.

Can rent go up during a lease in Ohio?

Usually not. A fixed-term lease sets the rent for the term, and the landlord cannot change it until the term ends unless the lease itself has a clause allowing a mid-term increase. The notice rules above matter when the term ends and a new term or a month-to-month tenancy begins. Read your lease for an escalation clause before assuming either way.

What can you do about a rent increase?

Start with the two checks on this page: was the notice in writing and long enough, and does your lease allow a change now. If the notice is valid, the question is whether the new rent is in line with the market, and that part is negotiable.
