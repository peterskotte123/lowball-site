Rent concession: what it means and what it is worth

A rent concession is a discount a landlord gives to fill a unit without lowering the rent written on the lease: a first month free, six weeks free, a move-in credit. On a $2,500 apartment with a 12-month lease, one month free means you really pay $2,292 a month.

What is a rent concession?

A rent concession is something of value a landlord gives a new or renewing tenant in place of a lower rent. The most common is free rent: a first month free, or a number of weeks free, on a lease of twelve months or more. Others are a one-time move-in credit or gift card, a waived application, administration or amenity fee, a reduced security deposit, or free parking for a period. The rent on the lease stays at the full figure, and the concession comes off somewhere else. Landlords, and large apartment buildings above all, prefer this to cutting the rent because the higher figure stays on the lease and on the building's rent roll, and because the next renewal is priced from it. Listings often call a concession a "special" or a "move-in special".

How much is first month free really worth?

Spread it over the lease. One month free on a 12-month lease at $2,500 takes $2,500 off a year that would have cost $30,000, so you pay $27,500 in total, or $2,292 a month on average. That is 8.3% off. Six weeks free on the same lease is worth $3,462 and brings the average to $2,212, or 11.5% off. The same free month on a 13-month lease is worth a little less per month, $2,308, because it is spread over one more month of paying. A $1,000 move-in credit on the 12-month lease works out to $2,417 a month. The average you get this way is called the net effective rent, and it is the number to compare between apartments, not the rent in the headline.

Do you get the free month up front or spread out?

Either, and the lease says which. With an up-front concession you pay nothing for the free period, usually the first month or the second, and the full lease rent every other month. With a spread, or prorated, concession the landlord divides the value across the lease and you pay the lower average figure every month. The total is the same. What differs is your monthly budget and what happens if you leave early: many leases with an up-front concession carry a clause that makes you repay it if you break the lease. Read the concession addendum for three things before signing: when the free period falls, whether the monthly payment is the full rent or the reduced one, and what is owed back if the lease ends early.

What happens to the rent at renewal?

The concession usually ends, and the increase is usually applied to the full rent on the lease. That is the part people miss. Take the $2,500 lease with one month free: you have been paying an average of $2,292. A renewal offer 3% above the lease rent is $2,575, which is a 12% jump from what you were really paying, though the landlord will describe it as 3%. In places with a cap on rent increases, check which figure the cap is measured from; the law, not this page, answers that. Before you sign a lease with a concession, ask in writing what rent a renewal would be based on. Is a concession better than a lower rent?

Compare the average you pay this year, then look at next year. A $2,500 apartment with one month free averages $2,292 a month, which beats a flat $2,300 apartment in year one. At renewal, with 3% added to each lease rent, the first becomes $2,575 and the second $2,369, a gap of $206 a month the other way. So a concession tends to favor a renter who plans to stay one lease term, and a lower rent favors one who plans to stay. Both are worth asking for. A landlord who will not move on the rent may still add free weeks, and one offering free weeks may agree to a lower rent in their place if you ask. How do you know if a listing with a special is a good deal?

Work out the average monthly figure with the calculator above, then compare that figure with what similar homes nearby are asking. A special is often a sign that a building has units it needs to fill, which is useful to know when you make an offer.
